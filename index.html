How this book was made

the method, the credits, and the care — for readers who want to know what they're holding

Why "scroll": an emaki (絵巻) is a Japanese narrative picture scroll, unrolled as it's read — story and teaching moving together on paper. This book's ink, paper, and unrolling page are built on that tradition.

  • Every chapter was written from a complete read of its transcript. No skimming, no sampling — every named story, framework, and number from the session is preserved, then expanded rather than summarized: the story first, the meaning beneath it, and only then how to practice it. One exception, named where it happens: the Bridge — chapter twelve — keeps a fraction of its session on purpose, quiet by design, with its source note listing what it set aside for the full deck.
  • The sequence is a hero's journey, not the playlist order. Fourteen katas re-sequenced from the first swing a young operator never takes, through craft and company, to the minds that hold wealth. The original playlist is linked below, unaltered.
  • Every chapter links back to its source video and credits it in full. This is an independent study companion — not affiliated with Varun Mayya or Aeos — built with respect for the material, and it exists to send readers back to the original.
  • Self-contained by design. Each chapter is a single HTML file — no build, no tracking, no accounts. The book remembers your place, your ink marks, and your practice lists on this device; installed from your browser, it reads entirely offline.

One confession: the compiler-narrator of the interludes is a device — the notes are real, the narrator is drawn, and the swing is yours.
